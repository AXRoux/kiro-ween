# Product: Trick or Read

A talking castle for blind and low-vision trick-or-treaters. Hold a treat up to the camera; the castle names it out
loud, warns about the allergens you said matter to you, and says whether the wrapper is still sealed.

## What counts as a treat
Candy, and the better-for-you alternatives people hand out instead: fig bars (Nature's Bakery), granola and fruit
bars, fruit snacks, fruit leather, raisins, pretzels, popcorn, crackers, cookies, chips, fruit pouches, juice boxes.
All of them get the same read, the same allergen check and the same wrapper check. Homemade or bare treats count as
"unwrapped" and get Beware, like an unwrapped candy.

## Who it is for
- **Primary:** a blind or low-vision person sorting their own Halloween haul, without asking anyone to read wrappers.
- **Secondary:** the parent, sibling or friend beside them, who sees the haunted interface and can confirm a warning.

## What matters, in order
1. **Never say something is safe when we do not know.** Allergen facts from a label (Open Food Facts) are stated as
   facts; anything the vision model infers is said as "likely" and ends with "ask someone to check the label".
2. **Hands-free.** Hold the candy still and it scans itself. Every action also has a key and a voice command.
3. **Speech first, sight second.** Everything the screen shows is spoken or announced to a screen reader.
4. **Findable by touch.** The interface is phone-shaped: one screen, the biggest target (the camera window) is the
   Read button, and the other actions are four big tiles that never move. No hidden pages, no custom gestures.
5. **The costume serves the function.** It is a phone app wearing a stone castle for a case, and every spooky element
   carries information: the portcullis says the camera is off, candlelight on the arch fills while you hold still, the
   gatehouse window and arrow slits burn in the verdict's colour, the island opens while the voice speaks.

## Verdicts
- **Beware:** an allergen on the user's list is listed or likely, or the wrapper is opened, torn or unwrapped.
- **Ask someone:** we could not read an allergen label, or the read was uncertain.
- **Safe:** sealed, and the label lists none of the user's allergens.
